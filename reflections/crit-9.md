# Crit 9 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough came from playing my own game and refusing to accept "fine". I
started the week by turning the wall into a Splatoon-style turf war: fixed
roller, team colours, rounds. It worked, but when I played it, it was still a
drawing app with a scoreboard. You painted from anywhere, so where you were
didn't matter. So I changed the rules: the player *is* the roller, with only
forward, back, turn left, turn right or stand still. Then the wall became a
place you have to travel across. Fading paint replaced rounds, and bots filled
the empty seats. When I looked at that version I noticed the camera was tilted
far more than I'd pictured, and the paint was blurry. I also realised the most
important thing: a wall is upright, so wet paint should run down it. That one
observation, gravity, turned decoration into a rule. Drips are real paint that
counts toward the score, because what you see has to be exactly what's scored.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

Last week I wrote that I care about interaction more than infrastructure. This
week showed me what that means in practice. My most useful contributions
weren't code. They were precise corrections after playing: "I didn't ask for
this angle", "it's too blurry", "a wall is vertical, so ink should drip". The
agent could build anything I described, so the quality of the result depended
on how clearly I could see what was wrong and say it. I want to be the kind of
developer who plays the thing, notices what feels off, and turns that feeling
into a concrete rule the build has to follow. I also learned to throw away
working versions when they aren't the right game. Rebuilding three times in one
week felt risky, but each version made the next decision obvious.
